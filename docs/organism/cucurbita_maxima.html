winter squash (Cucurbita maxima)

The closest species with at least 100 curator-checked (Swiss-Prot) proteins is cucumber (Cucumis sativus); the two lineages split 27 million years ago (Mya). In human proteins searched against a species that far away (chimpanzee, 6 Mya), the three tools with the best Fmax (the balance of finding domains and being right) were kmerseek; HH-suite (hhsearch); MMseqs2, three rounds, and kmerseek found 37% of the known Pfam domains. In the measured organism closest to this one, human (Homo sapiens), 15% of proteins had no hit from any sequence tool, so expect a share like that to stay without a label.

downloads this species' proteins and Swiss-Prot, runs nf-core/proteinannotator 1.1.0, then the three tools; needs a Unix computer with curl, Nextflow and Docker
# winter squash (Cucurbita maxima)

Three tools, in the order to run them

How this was measured: 998 human proteins were searched against the proteome of chimpanzee (Pan troglodytes), whose lineage split from human 6 Mya. Of the 15 relatives measured, its split age is the closest to this species' 27 Mya. Split age is a stand-in for how much proteins have changed; lineages change at different speeds. Counted: 1,399 Pfam domains in human proteins whose mean AlphaFold confidence (pLDDT, 0–100; AlphaFold DB calls 70 to 90 confident and above 90 very high) is 70-90. Tools are ranked by Fmax, the best balance of finding domains (recall) and being right (precision), 1 being perfect; the three differ by 0.122 in Fmax, a gap this one set of proteins cannot settle, so if cost matters pick the cheapest of them.

    Finds 37% of Pfam domains at this distance, and 55% of the domains it reports (its calls) are right; starts a found domain a median 23 residues from where the domain starts (measured at zebrafish, 430 Mya); finds 1% of short Swiss-Prot features 2–15 residues long, such as motifs and binding sites (5% of those calls right).

    Counted at the score cut-off that gives this tool its best Fmax: 0.445 on 1,399 domains; short features from 546 Swiss-Prot features. A found domain and its call overlap over a median 64% of the span they cover together.

What no tool on this board will find for you

15.1% of 20,600 proteins of human (Homo sapiens) had no hit from any sequence tool, even at a loose E-value below 1 (the E-value is the number of hits that size expected by chance). They were searched against Swiss-Prot with the organism's own group removed; we call these proteins dark.
