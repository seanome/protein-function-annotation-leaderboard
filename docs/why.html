This site measures, for every tool and every kind of organism, which proteins get a label and what the run costs, and tells each lab which tool to run. The first organism page will be the Pacific oyster's; it is not built yet, so there is no link to it.

Why ocean animals are dark

Science describes proteins where people work on them. Five species hold more reviewed proteins than all ocean animals put together, many times over.

The proteins that gave us underwater glue, antifreeze, a painkiller and the green light of cell biology were all found at the bench, by people, not by a tool. Three of the four are built from repeats or short peptides. On the leaderboard, the best tool finds 7 in 100 known features 2 to 15 residues long, against 28 in 100 features of any length. Repeats are found about as often as other features (30 in 100), and stretches with no fixed shape are not yet measured.

Who is behind it

  • Seanome Inc., a nonprofit.
  • The maintainers of nf-core/proteinannotator, the pipeline that labels a proteome's proteins.
  • If the proposed fork goes ahead (nothing agreed yet): the LAFA / CAFA group, who run functionbench.net (source).

Licence: the site's own repository has no licence file yet. Each tool keeps its own licence, listed on its tool card.
