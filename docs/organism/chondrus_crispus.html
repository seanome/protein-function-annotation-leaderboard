carragheen (Chondrus crispus)

The closest species with at least 100 curator-checked (Swiss-Prot) proteins is Gracilaria tenuistipitata; the two lineages split 363 million years ago (Mya). In human proteins searched against a species that far away (western clawed frog, 350 Mya), the three tools with the best Fmax (the balance of finding domains and being right) were phmmer; MMseqs2, three rounds; jackhmmer, and phmmer found 50% of the known Pfam domains. In the measured organism closest to this one, human (Homo sapiens), 15% of proteins had no hit from any sequence tool, so expect a share like that to stay without a label.

downloads this species' proteins and Swiss-Prot, runs nf-core/proteinannotator 1.1.0, then the three tools; needs a Unix computer with curl, Nextflow and Docker
# carragheen (Chondrus crispus)
# UniProt reference proteome UP000012073, 9,598 proteins
curl -L -o proteome.fasta.gz 'https://rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/stream?query=proteome%3AUP000012073&format=fasta&compressed=true'
# All reviewed Swiss-Prot proteins, the source of the labels (they include those of the nearest reviewed relative, Gracilaria tenuistipitata).
# Swiss-Prot has about 575,000 proteins; the board measured cost against one relative's proteome of about 20,000, so expect longer runs.
curl -L -o swissprot.fasta.gz 'https://ftp.uniprot.org/pub/databases/uniprot/current_release/knowledgebase/complete/uniprot_sprot.fasta.gz'
# nf-core/proteinannotator 1.1.0: Pfam, FunFam and InterProScan on your proteome.
# Version 1.1.0 has no option to run phmmer; MMseqs2, three rounds; jackhmmer; their commands follow.
printf 'id,fasta\nchondrus_crispus,proteome.fasta.gz\n' > samplesheet.csv
nextflow run nf-core/proteinannotator -r 1.1.0 -profile docker --input samplesheet.csv --outdir results

# -E 10.0 keeps every hit, as on the board. The board's numbers count calls above the score cut-off
# that gives each tool its best Fmax; filter the output before you take labels from it.

# phmmer (quay.io/biocontainers/hmmer:3.4--hb6cb901_4 (sha256:7a2b317b8d2fd3650b4924a8482cddeb940d4a0746c6a1501ff03ac1b7439e0c))
gunzip -kf proteome.fasta.gz swissprot.fasta.gz
phmmer --domtblout phmmer.domtbl --noali -E 10.0 --cpu 8 proteome.fasta swissprot.fasta > /dev/null

# MMseqs2, sensitivity 7, three rounds (quay.io/biocontainers/mmseqs2:18.8cc5c--hd6d6fdc_0 (sha256:3503bfe576d560e550df2872af86a1ad1bcc1c06cfb7caadd3e7a95649f5f0ef))
mmseqs createdb proteome.fasta.gz q_db
mmseqs createdb swissprot.fasta.gz t_db
mmseqs search q_db t_db result_s7_n3 tmp_s7_n3 --threads 16 -s 7 --num-iterations 3 --max-seqs 1000 -e 10.0
mmseqs convertalis q_db t_db result_s7_n3 mmseqs2_s7_3rounds.m8

# jackhmmer, three rounds (quay.io/biocontainers/hmmer:3.4--hb6cb901_4 (sha256:7a2b317b8d2fd3650b4924a8482cddeb940d4a0746c6a1501ff03ac1b7439e0c))
jackhmmer -N 3 --domtblout jackhmmer.domtbl --noali -E 10.0 --cpu 8 proteome.fasta swissprot.fasta > /dev/null

Three tools, in the order to run them

How this was measured: 998 human proteins were searched against the proteome of western clawed frog (Xenopus tropicalis), whose lineage split from human 350 Mya. Of the 15 relatives measured, its split age is the closest to this species' 363 Mya. Split age is a stand-in for how much proteins have changed; lineages change at different speeds. Counted: 748 Pfam domains in human proteins whose mean AlphaFold confidence (pLDDT, 0–100; AlphaFold DB calls 70 to 90 confident and above 90 very high) is 50-70. Tools are ranked by Fmax, the best balance of finding domains (recall) and being right (precision), 1 being perfect; the three differ by 0.030 in Fmax, a gap this one set of proteins cannot settle, so if cost matters pick the cheapest of them.
