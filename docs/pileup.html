Region pile-up

Where each tool's calls fall around 2,435 known Pfam domains on 998 human proteins, searching the zebrafish proteome (430 million years since the split).

Data from 15 Oct 2026, commit 4a4b1171 (source)

The 7 aligners cover 78% to 86% of each domain on average, and also 71% to 84% of the 100 residues either side: their calls run well past the domain's ends. kmerseek (gbmr4, k = 21, its best setting by Fmax) misses 83% of the domains. hmmscan, graded on its own models, covers 99% inside and 29% of the flanks.

How to read a panel: covered, missed, inside, flanks, edge sharpness

A call is a stretch of a human protein that a tool matched to a zebrafish protein carrying a Pfam domain. It names that domain and spans the whole matched stretch, which can be much wider than the domain. A residue is covered when a call naming that row's own Pfam family spans it, at any score the tool reported (E-value cutoff 10). Covered does not mean correctly placed: a call that runs far past the domain still covers it.

Each row shows 100 residues before the domain, then the domain stretched or squeezed into 100 equal steps, then 100 residues after it, as deepTools does for genes. The median domain is 89 aa, so one step is about 0.9 aa.

The numbers under each panel: missed is the share of domains with no covered residue inside (lower is better). Inside is the mean coverage inside the domain (higher is better). Flanks is the mean coverage in the 100 residues either side; for placing domain ends lower is better, though a second copy of the same domain nearby counts as covered too. Edge sharpness is how many residues the curve takes to fall from 0.8 to 0.2 at each end of the domain: fewer residues means a sharper edge. "None" means the curve never reaches 0.8 inside the domain, or never falls to 0.2 within 100 residues outside it; the chosen panel below says which.

Rows from one protein or one family are not independent, so the thin line repeats the curve with one domain per Pfam family (1,089 families).

Counts and sources

2,429 of the 2,435 domains have at least one residue inside their protein's sequence; the readouts use those. With one domain per Pfam family (1,089 families), the domain kept is the first by protein accession, then start. 9 Pfam domains run partly or wholly past the end of the protein sequence this run searched, so their Pfam coordinates belong to a different version of the sequence.
