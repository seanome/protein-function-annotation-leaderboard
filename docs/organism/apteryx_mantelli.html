North Island brown kiwi (Apteryx mantelli)

The closest species with at least 100 curator-checked (Swiss-Prot) proteins is chicken (Gallus gallus); the two lineages split 108 million years ago (Mya). In human proteins searched against a species that far away (cow, 89 Mya), the three tools with the best Fmax (the balance of finding domains and being right) were kmerseek; phmmer; MMseqs2, three rounds, and kmerseek found 32% of the known Pfam domains. In the measured organism closest to this one, human (Homo sapiens), 15% of proteins had no hit from any sequence tool, so expect a share like that to stay without a label.

downloads this species' proteins and Swiss-Prot, runs nf-core/proteinannotator 1.1.0, then the three tools; needs a Unix computer with curl, Nextflow and Docker
# North Island brown kiwi (Apteryx mantelli)
# UniProt reference proteome UP001652627, 28,723 proteins
curl -L -o proteome.fasta.gz 'https://rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/stream?query=proteome%3AUP001652627&format=fasta&compressed=true'
# All reviewed Swiss-Prot proteins, the source of the labels (they include those of the nearest reviewed relative, Gallus gallus).
# Swiss-Prot has about 575,000 proteins; the board measured cost against one relative's proteome of about 20,000, so expect longer runs.
curl -L -o swissprot.fasta.gz 'https://ftp.uniprot.org/pub/databases/uniprot/current_release/knowledgebase/complete/uniprot_sprot.fasta.gz'
# nf-core/proteinannotator 1.1.0: Pfam, FunFam and InterProScan on your proteome.
# Version 1.1.0 has no option to run kmerseek; phmmer; MMseqs2, three rounds; their commands follow.
printf 'id,fasta\napteryx_mantelli,proteome.fasta.gz\n' > samplesheet.csv
nextflow run nf-core/proteinannotator -r 1.1.0 -profile docker --input samplesheet.csv --outdir results

# -E 10.0 keeps every hit, as on the board. The board's numbers count calls above the score cut-off
# that gives each tool its best Fmax; filter the output before you take labels from it.

# kmerseek: its database needs build steps this page does not write out.
# The command the board ran is on its tool card: tools/kmerseek.html

# phmmer (quay.io/biocontainers/hmmer:3.4--hb6cb901_4 (sha256:7a2b317b8d2fd3650b4924a8482cddeb940d4a0746c6a1501ff03ac1b7439e0c))
gunzip -kf proteome.fasta.gz swissprot.fasta.gz
phmmer --domtblout phmmer.domtbl --noali -E 10.0 --cpu 8 proteome.fasta swissprot.fasta > /dev/null

# MMseqs2, sensitivity 7, three rounds (quay.io/biocontainers/mmseqs2:18.8cc5c--hd6d6fdc_0 (sha256:3503bfe576d560e550df2872af86a1ad1bcc1c06cfb7caadd3e7a95649f5f0ef))
mmseqs createdb proteome.fasta.gz q_db
mmseqs createdb swissprot.fasta.gz t_db
mmseqs search q_db t_db result_s7_n3 tmp_s7_n3 --threads 16 -s 7 --num-iterations 3 --max-seqs 1000 -e 10.0
mmseqs convertalis q_db t_db result_s7_n3 mmseqs2_s7_3rounds.m8

Three tools, in the order to run them

No AlphaFold models counted for this species, so the ranking uses all domains at this distance.

How this was measured: 998 human proteins were searched against the proteome of cow (Bos taurus), whose lineage split from human 89 Mya. Of the 14 relatives measured that have a TimeTree age, its split age is the closest to this species' 108 Mya. Split age is a stand-in for how much proteins have changed; lineages change at different speeds. Counted: 2,435 Pfam domains in human proteins of any AlphaFold confidence. Tools are ranked by Fmax, the best balance of finding domains (recall) and being right (precision), 1 being perfect; the three differ by 0.088 in Fmax, a gap this one set of proteins cannot settle, so if cost matters pick the cheapest of them.
