MMseqs2, three rounds

A profile of a protein family, built by MMseqs2 from its own hits over three rounds of search, as jackhmmer does.

Will it work for you?

  1. Is the closest same-family domain in the relative you will search at least 40% identical, for most of your proteins? If yes, Fmax here is 40–60% 0.241 (n = 563), 60–100% 0.300 (n = 1,289). If no, 20–30% 0.337 (n = 93), 30–40% 0.209 (n = 165). (Pfam truth set, raw calls, zebrafish (435 Mya) as the relative searched, setting sensitivity 7, 3 rounds; subsets with at least 30 true instances.)
  2. Are the features you care about longer than 60 residues? If yes, Fmax here is 61–120 aa 0.245 (n = 963), 121–250 aa 0.319 (n = 504), 251 aa or more 0.462 (n = 277). If no, 16–30 aa 0.028 (n = 271), 31–60 aa 0.085 (n = 420). (Pfam truth set, raw calls, zebrafish (435 Mya) as the relative searched, setting sensitivity 7, 3 rounds.)
  3. Can you spend about 0.0787 core-hours per 1,000 proteins for each relative you search? That is the measured mean per relative; see "Cost per 1,000 queries" below.

Best Fmax and cost

Under the Pfam truth set, raw calls, zebrafish (435 Mya) as the relative searched, its best setting is sensitivity 7, 3 rounds (s7): Fmax 0.324 over 2,435 true instances. Searching costs about $0.00351 per 1,000 queries (0.0787 core-hours), for each relative searched.

Can its weak hits be trusted?

When this tool's best hit on a protein is weak, is it right? It was measured on the dark set of four species, each searched against Swiss-Prot without its own clade. Dark proteins have no phmmer, jackhmmer or MMseqs2 hit at E ≤ 1; placed proteins are the rest. A best hit is right when the target shares a Pfam family with the query and the hit covers a Pfam domain of that family on the query. n counts the best hits whose target has any Pfam family. What it compares

A profile of a protein family, built by MMseqs2 from its own hits over three rounds of search, as jackhmmer does.

What it needs

  • sequence only: yes
  • 3D structure prediction: no
  • GPU: no

Reference searched

The relative's proteome (Quest for Orthologs reference proteomes, release 2020_04), as an MMseqs2 database.

How to read its score

Bit score: higher ranks first. The E-value cutoff was 10.

Known failure modes

  • At most 1,000 target proteins are kept per query (--max-seqs 1000). How often that cut hits: not measured.
  • Each round can pull in proteins from a different family. How often: not measured.

Cost per 1,000 queries

Setting s7, searching one relative (mean over the 15 relatives it searched): 0.0787 core-hours reserved per 1,000 queries (16 cores for the task's wall time), measured on Sherlock. Peak memory 1.1 GB. That is about $0.00351 at $0.044625 per CPU-hour. Building its databases took 0.00889 core-hours over 7 databases. A database is built once and reused, so this is not in the per-query cost. This covers the 7 database builds the task records hold; the search used 15 relatives.

The per-query cost counts the search only. The run recorded cost per tool and setting, summed over all relatives, so the cost for one relative is the mean.
