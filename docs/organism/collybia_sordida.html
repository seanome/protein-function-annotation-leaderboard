Collybia sordida

The closest species with at least 100 curator-checked (Swiss-Prot) proteins is Coprinopsis cinerea; the two lineages split 115 million years ago (Mya). In human proteins searched against a species that far away (mouse, 100 Mya), the three tools with the best Fmax (the balance of finding domains and being right) were kmerseek; phmmer; MMseqs2, three rounds, and kmerseek found 35% of the known Pfam domains. In the measured organism closest to this one, baker's yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae), 21% of proteins had no hit from any sequence tool, so expect a share like that to stay without a label.

downloads this species' proteins and Swiss-Prot, runs nf-core/proteinannotator 1.1.0, then the three tools; needs a Unix computer with curl, Nextflow and Docker
# Collybia sordida
# UniProt reference proteome UP000662569, 44 proteins
curl -L -o proteome.fasta.gz 'https://rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/stream?query=proteome%3AUP000662569&format=fasta&compressed=true'
# All reviewed Swiss-Prot proteins, the source of the labels (they include those of the nearest reviewed relative, Coprinopsis cinerea).
# Swiss-Prot has about 575,000 proteins; the board measured cost against one relative's proteome of about 20,000, so expect longer runs.
curl -L -o swissprot.fasta.gz 'https://ftp.uniprot.org/pub/databases/uniprot/current_release/knowledgebase/complete/uniprot_sprot.fasta.gz'
# nf-core/proteinannotator 1.1.0: Pfam, FunFam and InterProScan on your proteome.
# Version 1.1.0 has no option to run kmerseek; phmmer; MMseqs2, three rounds; their commands follow.
printf 'id,fasta\ncollybia_sordida,proteome.fasta.gz\n' > samplesheet.csv
nextflow run nf-core/proteinannotator -r 1.1.0 -profile docker --input samplesheet.csv --outdir results

# -E 10.0 keeps every hit, as on the board. The board's numbers count calls above the score cut-off
# that gives each tool its best Fmax; filter the output before you take labels from it.

# kmerseek: its database needs build steps this page does not write out.
# The command the board ran is on its tool card: tools/kmerseek.html

# phmmer (quay.io/biocontainers/hmmer:3.4--hb6cb901_4 (sha256:7a2b317b8d2fd3650b4924a8482cddeb940d4a0746c6a1501ff03ac1b7439e0c))
gunzip -kf proteome.fasta.gz swissprot.fasta.gz
phmmer --domtblout phmmer.domtbl --noali -E 10.0 --cpu 8 proteome.fasta swissprot.fasta > /dev/null

# MMseqs2, sensitivity 7, three rounds (quay.io/biocontainers/mmseqs2:18.8cc5c--hd6d6fdc_0 (sha256:3503bfe576d560e550df2872af86a1ad1bcc1c06cfb7caadd3e7a95649f5f0ef))
mmseqs createdb proteome.fasta.gz q_db
mmseqs createdb swissprot.fasta.gz t_db
mmseqs search q_db t_db result_s7_n3 tmp_s7_n3 --threads 16 -s 7 --num-iterations 3 --max-seqs 1000 -e 10.0
mmseqs convertalis q_db t_db result_s7_n3 mmseqs2_s7_3rounds.m8

Three tools, in the order to run them

Only 44 AlphaFold models counted for this species, fewer than 100, so the ranking uses all domains at this distance.

How this was measured: 998 human proteins were searched against the proteome of mouse (Mus musculus), whose lineage split from human 100 Mya. Of the 15 relatives measured, its split age is the closest to this species' 115 Mya. Split age is a stand-in for how much proteins have changed; lineages change at different speeds. Counted: 2,435 Pfam domains in human proteins of any AlphaFold confidence. Tools are ranked by Fmax, the best balance of finding domains (recall) and being right (precision), 1 being perfect; the three differ by 0.118 in Fmax, a gap this one set of proteins cannot settle, so if cost matters pick the cheapest of them.
