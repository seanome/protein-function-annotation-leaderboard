SCOPe40: does a tool rank a domain's relatives first?

Given one known domain, does the tool rank the domain's known relatives above the first unrelated hit?

Data from 20 Oct 2026, commit 7eca428b (source)

Table 1, on the leaderboard page, places annotations on human proteins by searching one relative's proteome, and scores where the ends of each call land. (Figures and tables are numbered across the whole site.) This page does something else: it ranks pairs of single structural domains from SCOPe, and never scores boundaries. SCOPe sorts domains into class.fold.superfamily.family; an id such as b.1.1.1 is class b (all beta), fold 1, superfamily 1, family 1. Domains in one superfamily are taken to share an ancestor; domains in one fold share a shape, with or without one. A family numbered 0 holds automated matches, domains placed in a superfamily but in no curated family; two domains in the same family 0 count as the same family. The set searched is SCOPe 2.08 at 40% identity, 15,177 domains. Every domain is searched against all of them. The queries are the 5,737 domains that have another member in the set at all three levels, as in Foldseek's benchmark (Methods). Foldseek's own set was SCOPe 2.01, 11,211 domains, so these numbers are not the paper's.

For one query, a true positive (TP) is a hit to another domain at the level being scored. At the family level that is the same family. At the superfamily level it is the same superfamily but a different family, and at the fold level the same fold but a different superfamily. A false positive (FP) is a hit to a domain of a different fold. Any other hit is not counted. Sort the hits by the tool's own score, best first, after dropping the query's hit to itself, and stop at the first FP. The sensitivity up to the first false positive is the TPs above that cut divided by the TPs the query has in the set: the domains it could find there. LoL-align, a structure aligner from the same groups as Foldseek, reuses Foldseek's benchmark with this denominator (Reifenrath et al. 2026). Foldseek's original script adds 1 to the bottom of the fraction at the superfamily and fold levels, because it leaves the query itself in those counts; Figure 19 shows both.

Over many queries, sort the queries by sensitivity, highest first, and plot sensitivity against the fraction of queries. The area under the curve (AUC) is the mean sensitivity over all queries: the AUC and the mean are the same number. (Foldseek's paper also says "area", for one query's own value; on this page AUC always means this area across queries.) Coverage is the share of queries with at least one hit at the settings the tool was run with. It says nothing about whether the hits are right: kmerseek on shuffled queries has coverage near 100% too. For the published files it is read from each row's counts: a query's first hit is either a TP at some level or an FP, so a row with no TP and no FP had no hits.

Every query counts. A query with no hits scores 0 and stays in every mean, every curve and every area. An earlier kmerseek claim, that it "outperforms Foldseek on SCOPe", was computed over only part of the queries: in its source, kmerseek found a family member for 3.3% of them, and the mean was taken over the queries both kmerseek and Foldseek returned hits for (Methods). That claim and its number are retired.

Inputs. In this site's pipeline, Foldseek runs on the SCOPe domain structures themselves (experimental coordinates from the PDB, not predicted); ProstT5 + Foldseek (ProstT5 is a protein language model that predicts Foldseek's 3Di shape letters from the sequence) and every other tool run on the domain sequences. The Foldseek and MMseqs2 rows shown here are not from that pipeline: they are read from per-query files that other authors published on SCOPe 2.08, kept in seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis (folder data/tea_scope40_rocx_files; the files do not name their run, its structures or its settings). Their scoring script dropped each self-hit. Their runs also searched a domain list with some 2.08 domains missing (family, superfamily or fold sizes smaller on 1,131 of their 5,713 rows), while kmerseek searched all 15,177, so kmerseek's rows and theirs are not on quite the same footing. 24 of the 5,737 queries are missing from both files; they score 0 and are listed under Table 3. kmerseek is scored from its own hit lists: the one-row-per-pair tables that its SCOPe 2.08 runs kept on Stanford's Sherlock cluster (seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis PR 107), 16 kmerseek settings, scored here with the same rules. Its hits are ranked by region E-value, the E-value of the best-matching region of a pair (smaller is better). A setting such as hsdm17.k4.c_max names the reduced alphabet (hsdm17, 17 letters), the k-mer length (k = 4) and the mismatch penalty used when a match is extended (c_max, the alphabet's largest log-odds penalty; c2, a penalty of 2). The chance level is the same kmerseek settings run on every query shuffled so that it keeps its count of every two-residue pair (dipeptide), seed 1. Not scored yet: phmmer; jackhmmer; MMseqs2, three rounds; HH-suite (hhsearch); ProstT5 + Foldseek. Their pipeline keeps per-query hit lists and is written but not launched (seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis PR 166).
