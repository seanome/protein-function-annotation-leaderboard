Dalmatian pelican (Pelecanus crispus)

The closest species with at least 100 curator-checked (Swiss-Prot) proteins is chicken (Gallus gallus); the two lineages split 96 million years ago (Mya). In human proteins searched against a species that far away (cow, 89 Mya), the three tools with the best Fmax (the balance of finding domains and being right) were kmerseek; HH-suite (hhsearch); phmmer, and kmerseek found 29% of the known Pfam domains. In the measured organism closest to this one, human (Homo sapiens), 15% of proteins had no hit from any sequence tool, so expect a share like that to stay without a label.

downloads this species' proteins and Swiss-Prot, runs nf-core/proteinannotator 1.1.0, then the three tools; needs a Unix computer with curl, Nextflow and Docker
# Dalmatian pelican (Pelecanus crispus)
# UniProt reference proteome UP000054150, 8,686 proteins
curl -L -o proteome.fasta.gz 'https://rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/stream?query=proteome%3AUP000054150&format=fasta&compressed=true'
# All reviewed Swiss-Prot proteins, the source of the labels (they include those of the nearest reviewed relative, Gallus gallus).

curl -L -o swissprot.fasta.gz 'https://ftp.uniprot.org/pub/databases/uniprot/current_release/knowledgebase/complete/uniprot_sprot.fasta.gz'

nextflow run nf-core/proteinannotator -r 1.1.0 -profile docker --input samplesheet.csv --outdir results

Three tools, in the order to run them

How this was measured: 998 human proteins were searched against the proteome of cow (Bos taurus), whose lineage split from human 89 Mya. Of the 14 relatives measured that have a TimeTree age, its split age is the closest to this species' 96 Mya. Split age is a stand-in for how much proteins have changed; lineages change at different speeds. Counted: 1,399 Pfam domains in human proteins whose mean AlphaFold confidence (pLDDT, 0–100; AlphaFold DB calls 70 to 90 confident and above 90 very high) is 70-90. Tools are ranked by Fmax, the best balance of finding domains (recall) and being right (precision), 1 being perfect; the three differ by 0.069 in Fmax, a gap this one set of proteins cannot settle, so if cost matters pick the cheapest of them.

What no tool on this board will find for you

15.1% of 20,600 proteins of human (Homo sapiens) had no hit from any sequence tool, even at a loose E-value below 1 (the E-value is the number of hits that size expected by chance). They were searched against Swiss-Prot with the organism's own group removed; we call these proteins dark. Of the five organisms measured, this one is closest to this species: their lineages meet at Amniota.
